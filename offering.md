## Who This Is For

Team leads, program managers, and engineering managers who report on work
that spans more than one team and more than one tool:

- The weekly status or sprint review means an afternoon of pulling numbers from
  Jira, a database, a spreadsheet, and a dashboard — then pasting them into slides
- Leadership asks a cross-team question ("which teams are carrying the most
  unplanned work?") and answering it is a mini-project every time
- Every team tracks things slightly differently, so the roll-up is manual and
  nobody fully trusts it

If two of those three sound familiar, we should talk.

## Integrations You Can Re-Run, Not One-Off Scripts

Each connector does one job — pull sprint tickets, query the warehouse, read a
SharePoint list — and returns the same shape every time. Run it Monday and you
get Monday's numbers: no duplicates, no half-finished state, nothing to clean
up if it runs twice. That is what makes the second report cheap: a new question
becomes a new combination of pieces you already own, not a new project.

Everything is delivered as code you own, running on your infrastructure — no
SaaS resale, no subscription lock-in, no slideware.

## What Gets Built On Top

- A weekly cross-team status summary, written from the real numbers

## AI Is Not the Product

Most reporting already has all the information it needs — it's just spread
across systems and collected by hand. The durable wins come from connecting
those systems, making the collection repeatable, and making the result easy to
ask questions of. Modern AI models are a flexible interface on top; the value
underneath is the integration.
